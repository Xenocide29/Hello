# My Journey with an Old Laptop

Hello! I'm **Art Xenos D. Guadalquiver**. I'm a struggling **2nd Year Computer Science student** who, honestly, has no passion for this course. There are many times when I question myself if Computer Science is really meant for me. There are days when programming feels too difficult, when I get frustrated with my mistakes, and when I feel like I'm falling behind compared to other students. But despite all of that, I still keep moving forward.

I'm continuing this journey because I want to have a better future and, most importantly, I want to make my parents proud. I may not be the best Computer Science student, and I may not always understand everything immediately, but I believe that continuing to move forward is already a form of progress.

The device I currently use, and maybe the same device I will continue using until the day I graduate, is my **HP Pavilion dv7-2012TX** laptop. This laptop was manufactured way back in **2009**, which means it is already more than a decade old. By today's standards, it is definitely considered an old machine. It has been with me through many assignments, projects, activities, and late nights of studying.

This laptop has a lot of problems. It is slow, it lags frequently, and sometimes it takes a long time just to open a program. It sometimes disconnects itself from the internet, and the wireless network adapter occasionally stops working completely. The **Enter key is also damaged**, which makes typing and programming more difficult than it should be. Its battery only lasts around **30 to 45 minutes**, so I usually have to keep it connected to a charger. It is also loud, especially when the fan starts working hard, and it gets extremely hot after being used for a while.

On top of all that, it runs **Windows 8.1**, an operating system that is already outdated for many of the applications and tools that we use in college. Because of its limitations, I cannot simply install newer systems or software that require more modern hardware and operating systems.

This has become a real problem for me as a Computer Science student. There are times when I get behind on assignments because my laptop cannot properly run the software that we are required to use. There are even situations where I cannot submit an assignment at all because I don't have the necessary tools available on my own computer. One example is **Cisco Packet Tracer**, which we use for our networking subject. Because my laptop cannot properly support the software and newer requirements, I sometimes have no choice but to borrow a computer from my classmates whenever they are available.

It can be embarrassing and frustrating at times. While other students can simply open their laptops and start working, I sometimes have to think first about whether my own laptop can handle the task. I have to find alternatives, wait for someone to lend me a device, or work around the limitations of hardware that is already very old.

But despite all of these problems, I cannot simply hate this laptop.

This old machine has also helped me in many ways.

It has allowed me to write countless assignments, practice programming, research topics, create projects, and complete school requirements. It was there when I was still in my first year of college. In its own limited way, it helped me survive my first year of Computer Science.

There was even a time when I managed to download an **older version of Visual Studio Code** that could still run on my laptop. It may not have been the newest version, but it was enough for me to continue practicing and working on my projects.

With that outdated version of VS Code and this old laptop, I was still able to create something that I am proud of: **my own personal portfolio website**. I created it for academic purposes and published it through another GitHub account at **[artxenos.github.io](https://artxenos.github.io)**.

It may seem like a small achievement, especially when compared to the projects made using modern computers and powerful development environments. But for me, it means something more. I was able to create a portfolio using a machine that was already old before I even started college. I had to work with what I had, find compatible software, and make the most out of the limited resources available to me.

That experience reminded me that sometimes, having limited resources does not necessarily mean that you cannot create something.

You just have to find another way.

Every time this laptop freezes, I wait.

Every time it becomes extremely hot, I let it cool down.

Every time the internet disconnects, I try to reconnect.

Every time the fan becomes loud, I just continue working.

Every time a program takes too long to open, I remind myself that at least it still opens.

And every time I manage to finish something despite all of these problems, I feel a little bit proud.

It may be an old laptop, but it has become part of my journey as a Computer Science student.

For now, I'm holding on to this laptop even though it troubles me. I know that someday I will probably have to replace it, especially as the requirements of my course become more demanding. But until that day comes, I will continue using it and making the most out of what I have.

This laptop may not be powerful.

It may not have the latest hardware.

It may not be able to run everything I need.

It may not even be able to keep up with me anymore.

But somehow, it has kept up enough to help me reach **2nd Year Computer Science**.

It helped me get through my first year. It helped me practice programming. It helped me create projects. It helped me build my portfolio. And even with all its problems, it continues to help me study today.

Maybe someday I'll finally have a better computer. Maybe someday I'll be able to run the newest development tools without worrying about compatibility, lag, overheating, or whether the software will even install.

But until then, I'll keep using this one.

I don't know what the next years of this course will look like. I don't know if this laptop will survive until graduation, and I don't even know if I will always feel motivated to continue Computer Science. There will probably be more difficult subjects, more complicated programming problems, more sleepless nights, and more moments when I question whether I can actually do this.

But I'm still here.

I'm still learning.

I'm still trying.

And I'm still moving forward.

I may be struggling, but I haven't given up.

**I'm still to do better.**
